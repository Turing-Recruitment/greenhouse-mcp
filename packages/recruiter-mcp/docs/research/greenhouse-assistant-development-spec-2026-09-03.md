# Greenhouse assistant development spec: recruiters and People Ops, one build

Date: 2026-09-03. Audience: Sam Vangelos, as the gate on each slice. This is the plan that turns the two research programs of 2026-09-02 (the People Ops pack and the recruiter side, six documents in this folder) into one sequenced build. It is a specification, not an implementation: nothing here has been coded, and every slice below waits on Sam's GO. The shape is the gated-build one this repo already uses: each sub-slice names its change with file and line, its tests, the gate that lets it ship, its rollback, and its effort, and the shared prerequisites go first because both audiences are blocked on the same four defects.

## 1. Where the build starts

Production is the Cloud Run service `greenhouse-recruiter-mcp-prod` in us-west1, revision 00005-5tf, built from merge `e5d92aeb` (PR #2, 2026-09-02). An entitled session sees 66 tools: 44 read tools from a 66-tool read catalog, and 22 write tools (11 preview/apply pairs). The suite stood at 1418 of 1418 tests at that merge. Three audit children are still open under CLO-269 and all three sit on the critical path of both research programs: site admins bypass the private-candidate gate (CLO-273), org-wide actors get a confirmation ceremony instead of an answer (CLO-274), and unrecognised questions fail closed with no reads (CLO-275). Sam's standing rule is that every blocker is fixed before the connector is announced to the team, so those three are week one.

Four rulings Sam made on 2026-09-02 are inputs, not open questions, and the slices cite them rather than re-raising them: candidate names, email addresses and phone numbers pass on the candidate row (deployed); teammate email addresses stay withheld from job-scoped recruiters and pass for site admins and operators; private notes, private scorecard notes and private custom-field values stay withheld on the analysis surface even from permission holders; and Kelsey Connolly and Eduardo Beck both hold the Greenhouse permission "Can create and view private candidates".

## 2. The three lines every slice keeps

Nothing is fabricated: every approximation is labeled as one at the point of use, with its denominator and window in words. Nothing silently grants a read that the org's own Greenhouse permissions deny; where the team wants an org-wide view it is built as an explicit mode that says so in every answer. And expansive is the default: any withheld field, capped read, or refused question in the slices below cites a specific external constraint, and a narrowing that cannot cite one is treated as a defect to remove. Three constraints in this spec pass that test. The EEOC and demographic endpoints return per-person rows where Greenhouse's own report returns only aggregates (`D/0089-get_v3-eeoc.md:7`), so they stay unbound until an aggregate-only recipe exists. Private candidates are hidden from users without the user-specific permission in Greenhouse itself (support article 115002695663), so the server hides them too. Sam's two projection rulings above are product calls and carry his name as the citation.

## 3. The eight open decisions, stated as assumptions

Each research brief ended with four decisions for Sam. The build below assumes the answer given here for each; if Sam rules otherwise, the paragraph says what moves.

#### People Ops 1: the starting pair

The build assumes offer outcomes first, then time to hire and time to fill. These are the two questions the audit caught the assistant promising and not delivering (the tool description's first example is offer acceptance rate; CLO-274 reproduced the refusal live), they share one read (accepted offers by `resolved_at`) and one helper (the reconciliation line), and the TI time-in-process census gives both a published number to reproduce as a gate. A different pair delays the gate, because no other recipe has a prior number to check against.

#### People Ops 2: who the pack runs for

The build assumes the pack runs for everyone, and every hire-denominated number carries the count of hires withheld because they are private candidates the asker cannot see in Greenhouse. The count is already computed: the read plane reports `privacyWithheld` on every list read (`C/index.ts:766-781`) precisely because an aggregate count discloses no individual. Restricting the pack to permission holders would be a narrowing with no external citation, since the scoped recruiter's 201 visible hires are what Greenhouse itself shows them. If Sam prefers permission-only, the change is one guard in the hire-facts helper (slice H0) and nothing else moves.

#### People Ops 3: the demographic mode

The build assumes it is deferred until a People Leadership request names the report, with one fifteen-minute check done now, during H0, on whether the service credential's token carries the `harvest:eeoc:list` scope at all. The mode is a week of governance and tests against a constraint that is real (the vendor's own page says the endpoint is row-level where the app is aggregate-only), and no request for it exists. Deferring a build for want of demand is sequencing, not timidity; the external constraint is what shapes it when it is built.

#### People Ops 4: the HRIS join table

The build assumes it comes after tier one, and outside this repo. Retention, actual starts and band-versus-offer are computed in the analytics platform against a HiBob extract, never inside the MCP (the capability map's section C), the join is candidate email to employee record with manual fallbacks, and the sponsor question is open since the head-of-TA change on 2026-08-17. Tier one answers 12 of the 16 People Ops asks without it. If Sam wants it now, it starts as its own week in the analytics repo in parallel with week three and changes nothing here.

#### Recruiter 1: the daily desk and the hidden-tools flip

The build assumes both, in the order flip then desk, across weeks two and three. The flip is two and a half days and closes six of the twenty traced questions on its own; the desk recipe is three to four days and closes the four questions recruiters ask every morning. The flip goes first because the desk's interview section wants `search_my_job_interviews` for durations and the rejection-email slice wants `email_templates` bound.

#### Recruiter 2: per-recruiter attribution before new write kinds

The build assumes attribution is fixed first. Every write today lands in Greenhouse's activity feed under the integration's name, because production sets neither `GREENHOUSE_ACTION_ATTRIBUTION_MODE` nor the probe flag (`A/greenhouse.ts:218-227`; verified on the live revision). The write plane's own rule is that every change carries the recruiter's name, and each new kind shipped before the flip multiplies the misattributed rows. The flip needs the token probe (`A/token-probe.ts:4-40`) to pass first, because the read and action planes share one Harvest client and that client's tokens have single-active semantics; slice P4 runs it.

#### Recruiter 3: rejection email as an option

The build assumes it is offered, as an optional input that raises the action to the high-impact tier when present, defaults to none, and schedules the send rather than firing it immediately. The app sends this email today; Greenhouse's own connector allows rejection behind a confirmation; and the documented incidents are recipient-set and template errors on bulk sends, which the plane's one-candidate-at-a-time preview already excludes. One thing is unverified and is checked in the sandbox slice: whether a scheduled rejection email can be cancelled from the app before it goes out. If it cannot, the delay is a review window only and the preview says so.

#### Recruiter 4: the sandbox stage-move test

The build assumes it runs now, in week two. The stage move is already live for anyone holding `can_apply_high_impact`, it has never been applied against the real tenant, and two details of the call are unverified (no `email_from_user_id` is passed, and `from_stage_id` receives an application-stage row id). Half a day on a test candidate marked Do Not Email settles both before any recruiter applies it in anger. No separate Greenhouse sandbox tenant is on record here, so the test runs on a test requisition in the production tenant; that is an assumption to confirm.

## 5. Shared prerequisites, week one

### P1. Org-wide actors get an answer, with the scope said out loud (CLO-274)

A site admin or operator asking any question today lands in the resolver and is forced to confirm, because `resolvePlannerScope` routes every `scopeKind !== "jobs"` actor with no explicit scope through `resolveJobScope` (`S/tools/question-answer.ts:1172-1201`), and the resolver forces confirmation for an admin on anything but exact ids (`(isAdmin && !exactPath)`, `S/resolvers/job-scope/resolver.ts:437`). The comment at `question-answer.ts:1175-1178` gives the reason as "never allowed to silently run org-wide", and the fix keeps the word that matters, which is silently. For a `kind:"all"` actor with no owner intent, no resolver params and no requisition named in the question, the planner runs the recipes over the actor's whole permitted set and attaches a header `{source: "permission_scope", scope_label: "all N jobs you can see in Greenhouse (org-wide)", job_count: N}`, so every answer says its scope in its first line. The resolver is used only when the question carries a title, alias, requisition id or role family. The same change lands in `resolveAnalysisContext` (`S/resolution/analysis-context.ts:122-129`) for direct `analyze_*` calls. At `resolver.ts:437` the admin clause is removed from `needsConfirmation`; `admin_scope` stays as a reason code that becomes a warning on the resolved handle, and the collision, closed-job, confidential-job and stale-index paths are untouched, since those protect scope-correctness for everyone. The `operator_site_admin` mode text in `S/resolvers/job-scope/capabilities.ts:400-409` changes from `auto_confirm_allowed: false` to true with a sentence saying answers run org-wide and the scope is disclosed on each.

Tests are red first in `test/question-answer-resolution.test.ts`, `test/job-scope-resolver.test.ts` and `test/analysis-scope-integration.test.ts`: an all-access actor asking "what is our offer acceptance rate this quarter across all open reqs" gets an answer with the org-wide header and no `resolution_required`; the same actor asking "where are candidates stuck in my reqs" gets owner scope with no confirmation; a narrow recruiter's behaviour is byte-identical to today (the existing suite is the lock); an admin naming a title that matches four jobs still gets a confirmation, because multiple matches confirm for everyone. The gate is the three CLO-274 reproductions re-run live as Sam through a hosted-client session on Cloud Run, each returning a number. Rollback is a code revert; no migration, no environment change.

### P2. Site admins see private candidates only when Greenhouse says they can (CLO-273)

A `kind:"all"` scope with no confidential exclusions returns the raw response before the row filter and before the privacy gate (`C/index.ts:704-716`), and on the filtered path `privateCapableJobIds` is every job (`:729-731`), so the gate at `:1020-1053` answers yes on every row. Greenhouse does not grant private-candidate access to site admins implicitly; it is a user-specific permission with no API surface, so the server needs an attestation it can read. Migration 0008 adds `private_candidates_attested boolean not null default false`, `private_candidates_attested_at timestamptz` and `private_candidates_attested_by text` to `recruiter_identity_directory` (schema at `packages/recruiter-mcp/supabase/migrations/0001_recruiter_identity_directory.sql:9-20`), and the permission provider carries the flag onto the `{kind:"all"}` scope (the shape at `C/index.ts:480-482`). The raw fast path at `:704` is taken only when the flag is true; otherwise the read goes through the filter with `privateCapableJobIds` empty, which withholds private rows and reports the count, exactly as a job-scoped recruiter without the permission is treated today. The write-plane fence inherits it, because `S/action-visibility.ts:121` runs the same pipeline. Three rows are attested on day one: Sam (5085047004), Kelsey (5182584004) and Eduardo (5281417004), the last two on their own attestation of 2026-09-02, written through the access CLI.

Red tests in `test/private-candidate-coverage.test.ts`, `test/authorization-boundaries.test.ts` and `test/action-visibility.test.ts`: an unattested all-access session's `search_my_candidates` withholds private rows with `privacyWithheld` disclosed; its `get_my_candidate` on a private id reports what a nonexistent id reports (the existence-suppression rule at `C/index.ts:766-781`); an attested session is byte-identical to today; a preview against a private target from an unattested session returns `TARGET_NOT_VISIBLE`. The gate is migration 0008 applied through the management API, the three rows present, and Kelsey's live session returning private hires. Rollback is a code revert; the migration is additive and the rows are inert without the code. Cost note: an unattested all-access read loses the fast path and pays per-row privacy joins, which today affects nobody, since every enrolled all-access user is attested.

### P3. Recipe windows mean one thing, and a question's own window reaches them

Three recipes apply their window differently from what their summaries claim. `pipeline_quality` passes `window_start` nowhere (`S/tools/pipeline-quality.ts:126`, `:292`) and then reports it as `freshness_window_start`; the fix names the recipe's real shape, a snapshot as of `window_end` with weekly inflow buckets, applies `window_start` to those buckets only (`:225-228`), and drops the freshness label. The two scorecard recipes send a server-side `created_at ≥ window_start` floor (`S/tools/scorecard-accountability.ts:86`; `S/tools/interview-feedback-drag.ts:97`) under an in-memory filter on `interviewed_at ?? submitted_at` (`:305`, `:357`), so a scorecard created when the interview was scheduled, two weeks before the window, and interviewed inside it is never read. The floor becomes `window_start` minus 90 days, a bounded read, the in-memory filter is authoritative, and a row with neither timestamp is excluded and counted as `missing_basis_timestamp` instead of passing as in-window. Separately, `parseQuestionTimeWindow` (`S/tools/question-answer.ts:720-751`) is applied only on the planned-domain path (`:805-836`); the recipe path (`:87-147`) receives a window only when the caller passes one, so "this month" is silently ignored. The fix forwards the parsed window as `window_start` and `window_end` to every selected recipe when no explicit window was given, and adds the same disclosure line the planned path already emits.

Red tests in `test/pipeline-quality.test.ts`, `test/scorecard-accountability.test.ts`, `test/interview-feedback-drag.test.ts` and `test/question-answer.test.ts`: a scorecard created 20 days before the window and interviewed inside it is counted; a question carrying "this month" reaches the recipe as a window and the summary discloses it; the pipeline summary no longer claims a window it does not apply. The gate is question 19 of the twenty ("who hasn't submitted their scorecard for yesterday's interviews") returning yesterday's set live. Rollback is a code revert.

### P4. Writes carry the recruiter's name, and an assignee must be able to open the application

Two fixes, one in code and one in the environment. `application_assignment_change` checks that the proposed user is active (`A/actions/application-assignment.ts:54`) and never that they can open the job, whereas the owner-change action already calls `assertUserMayOwnJob` (`A/actions/shared.ts:111-121`); the assignment action switches to the same check and denies with `USER_JOB_PERMISSION_DENIED`. For attribution, Harvest v3's token endpoint accepts a `sub` claim naming the user to act on behalf of (`D/0010-generate-token.md:57-61`), and the gateway already mints per-user tokens when the mode is `per_human` (`A/greenhouse.ts:62-105`, `:117`, `:157`). The mode is blocked until `GREENHOUSE_ACTION_PER_HUMAN_TOKEN_PROBE_PASSED` is exactly true (`:223-224`), because the read plane and the action plane share one Harvest client (the `9e99…` fingerprint recorded on CLO-269 on 2026-09-02) and that client's tokens have single-active semantics, so a per-user mint might revoke the read plane's service token. `probePerHumanTokens` (`A/token-probe.ts:4-40`) mints concurrent tokens for two real users, checks each token's subject, and verifies all of them still work after a fresh mint. It runs once against production with Sam and Eduardo as the two subjects. If it passes, the Cloud Run revision gets `GREENHOUSE_ACTION_ATTRIBUTION_MODE=per_human` and the probe flag; if it fails, a separate Harvest client for the action plane is minted in Dev Center first, which the operational lens already wants for the shared-credential race, and the probe is re-run against that client. The preview envelope gains one sentence naming whose name the write will carry.

Red test in `packages/action-mcp/test/actions.test.ts`: assigning an application to a user with no permission row on its job is denied. The probe's own tests exist (`test/token-probe.test.ts`). The gate is the probe's output recorded on CLO-269, then one live note on a test candidate appearing in Greenhouse's activity feed under Sam's name, with no rise in 401 responses in Cloud Run logs over the following half hour. Rollback is the environment of the prior revision; the assignee check reverts with the code.

### P5. The model-facing text says what the code does

Six known contradictions, each a small edit and together half a day. `get_recruiting_capabilities` tells every session "No write/admin tools" (`S/resolvers/job-scope/capabilities.ts:417`) and that "Offer/compensation signals remain unavailable" (`:328`); the excluded list becomes a function of the session's granted tools and profile, and the offer sentence goes. The users and referrers and offers descriptions describe the old projection (`S/tools/evidence.ts:89-90`, `:92`, `:95`); users becomes "colleague emails are returned to site admins and operators only", which states Sam's ruling, referrers says `user_id` passes, and offers says compensation custom fields pass unless the field definition is private. The unrecognised-question message and the `recipes` parameter description omit `rejection_reason_drift` (`S/tools/question-answer.ts:357`, `:365`; `S/tools/register.ts:407`); both are generated from the `RECIPES` list so they cannot drift again. Every search tool publishes a `cursor` parameter (`S/tools/evidence.ts:186-188`) that the engine does not honour; it is removed from the model schema. And the registrar drops the human titles the action package ships (`S/tools/register.ts:466-486`), so Claude Desktop's permission prompt shows `apply_application_stage_move`; the registrar gains a title slot and passes them through.

Tests: `test/capabilities-knowledge.test.ts`, `test/param-contract.test.ts`, `test/evidence.test.ts`, and `test/recipe-availability-contract.test.ts` locks the message to the registered recipe list. The gate is `get_recruiting_capabilities` on a 66-tool hosted session listing the 22 write tools. Rollback is a code revert.

### P6. Unknown questions get a labeled composite instead of a refusal (CLO-275)

When no recipe keyword matches and no broad phrase is present, the planner returns `missing_domain` with zero reads (`S/tools/question-answer.ts:326-374`). The comment at `:581-582` defends it as avoiding "a confident five-recipe composite"; the alternative to a confident composite is a labeled one. On `selected.length === 0` the planner runs `BROAD_DIAGNOSTIC_RECIPES` under the existing analysis deadline with `answer.mode: "approximate_composite"`, `domain_recognized: false`, and a first line saying no recipe matched the question and naming the ones that ran. Truncation stays disclosed the way every recipe already discloses it. The cost to watch is an org-wide actor after P1: six recipes over the whole tenant, where `pipeline_quality` alone reads every application on every permitted job and will hit the read engine's page ceiling (`S/read-all.ts:185`); the composite must return inside the analysis budget with partial completeness rather than `TOOL_TIMEOUT`, and that is the gate, measured live as Sam. Red test in `test/question-answer.test.ts`: an unmatched question yields non-empty analyses and the disclosure line. Rollback is a code revert.

### R3. Find a candidate by name (week five)

Three of the twenty questions start from a person's name, and neither `/v3/candidates` nor `/v3/applications` filters on one. The requisition resolver already solves this shape for jobs, so `S/resolvers/candidate-scope/` is its twin: an inventory keyed on `first_name`, `last_name` and `preferred_name`, a resolver returning matches with their application context (job title, stage, status), the same signed-handle contract, and a `candidate_handle` accepted by `get_my_candidate`, `search_my_applications` and every `preview_*` tool that takes an application. Two names that match the same query are listed, never guessed. Scope bounds the index: a job-scoped recruiter's candidates are the ones with an application on their permitted jobs; an all-access actor cannot index 700,000 applications, so the index for them is candidates with an application on an open job plus any candidate active in the last 180 days, and the answer says that bound in words. The `email` filter on candidates, hidden at `S/harvest-v3-registry.ts:315`, is exposed, since the row now carries the email under Sam's ruling. Private candidates pass through P2's gate before they can match, so an actor without the permission sees no match and no hint that one exists.

Tests: new `test/candidate-scope-resolver.test.ts` and handle tests on the `job-scope-handle.test.ts` pattern; a red test that a private candidate's name from a non-permitted recruiter returns no match with the existence-suppression semantics; homonym and preferred-name cases. The gate is "where is Jane Doe in process" resolving live for a pilot recruiter to the right application. Rollback is a code revert.

### R5. Six analyses in the market's words (week six)

The renames follow the external research's verdicts, with the old names kept as aliases for one release so nothing a recruiter has learned stops working. Stage latency keeps its name, adds a median beside the mean, turns the seven-day threshold into a per-stage-class goal the caller can override, and labels Application Review separately. Scorecard accountability becomes feedback completion and adds the rate. Interview feedback drag becomes feedback turnaround, measured in hours with a warning tier at 24 hours and a missed tier at 48, the current two-day default restated. Pipeline quality keeps the status mix and drops the stale-by-last-activity component (`S/tools/pipeline-quality.ts:296`, `:309`), which matches no vendor's definition; pass-through by stage arrives with PO7. Source quality becomes source of hire, windowed on the hire date from H0, grouped by source category (`type.name`) so the six LinkedIn source ids stop ranking as six rows, with an application-to-hire rate per source. Rejection reason drift becomes rejection reasons, with drift as an explicit comparison of this window against the prior one and a stage dimension from the current `application_stages` row, which folds the People Ops "rejection reasons by stage" ask into this slice. Router regexes, the capabilities catalog and every description change with the names, and each description states the clock, the window and the denominator in words, which is the convention Ashby's catalog set and the one the People Ops recipes follow.

Tests: the six existing recipe test files, alias tests, and `test/recipe-availability-contract.test.ts`. The gate is every old name still answering through its alias, and a snapshot of the six descriptions reviewed by Sam. Rollback is a code revert.

### R4. Finish the writes (weeks seven and eight)

R4a adds `rejection_email` as an optional object on the rejection action (`A/actions/application-rejection.ts:17-21`): `email_template_id` from the newly bound templates, `send_email_at` defaulting to 24 hours after apply, and `email_from_user_id` set to the acting recruiter under P4. When present the action is high-impact and the effects line names the template, the send time and the recipient; when absent the body is unchanged (`:163-170`) and the rejection stays silent, which remains the default. R4b adds five thin kinds, each a clone of an existing pair: `application_hire` (`POST /v3/applications/{id}/hire` with `opening_id`, `start_date` and `close_reason_id`, high-impact because the hired status is what fires HRIS integrations), `prospect_convert` (`convert_to_candidate`), `application_create` (a second requisition for an existing candidate), `candidate_tag_change` (applied tags create and delete), and `rejection_reason_correct` (`PATCH /v3/rejection_details/{id}`, which fixes a reason without re-rejecting). R4c adds interview record create, update and delete (`POST`, `PATCH`, `DELETE /v3/interviews`, which record a loop the calendar already holds and send nothing) with a new `interview` fence target in `S/action-visibility.ts:45-53`, and the approval chain (`approval_flows` create, `request_approvals`, which emails approvers and is therefore high-impact, and the two replace endpoints), completing the offer flow that `offer_create` explicitly stops short of.

Tests: `packages/action-mcp/test/actions.test.ts`, `action-variants.test.ts`, `preview-legibility.test.ts`, `fence-structure.test.ts`, and `test/plane-parity.test.ts` in the recruiter package. The gate for each kind is a preview and one apply on the test candidate, an audit object present in the GCS store, and the change appearing in Greenhouse under the recruiter's name. Rollback is the capability environment variables that already default to the shipped kinds (`A/env.ts:138-141`), which drop a kind without a deploy, then a code revert.

## 7. People Ops track

### H0. Hire facts and the reconciliation line (week two)

Every People Ops recipe needs the same thing first: one definition of a hire, and the other two counts printed beside it. Harvest v3 has no hire timestamp on the application; the hire date is `offers.resolved_at` on an accepted offer, which is the definition every published report already uses, and on this tenant the accepted-offer count, the `status=hired` application count and the openings-closed-by-hire count disagree. `S/tools/hire-facts.ts` provides `readHireSet(scope, window)`, which reads `list_offers` with `status=Accepted`, `current_only=true`, the scope's `job_ids` and a `resolved_at` range, plus the full version chain when a caller needs the per-extension denominator, and `reconciliationLine(scope, window)`, which returns the three counts for the window and the number of hires withheld as private candidates, summed from `privacyWithheld` across the read's pages. The facts register in `S/tools/analysis-fact-metrics.ts` with a `hire_count` metric in `METRIC_REGISTRY` (`S/metrics.ts:72-297`) carrying `requiredFields`. The same slice fixes the planner's offer path: `executePlannedDomain` reads offers with empty parameters (`S/tools/question-answer.ts:753-770`), so superseded versions inflate its total, and its window field is `sent_on` (`:714`); it becomes `current_only=true` on `resolved_at`. Two probes ride along, each a few minutes: whether the tenant's base-pay offer field is flagged private (`search_my_custom_fields?field_type=offer`), which decides whether PO10 exists on the recruiter surface, and whether the service credential's token carries `harvest:eeoc:list`, which decides whether the deferred demographic mode is possible at all.

Tests: new `test/hire-facts.test.ts` and `test/metrics.test.ts`. The gate is a cross-check against a published number: the accepted-offer count for one reporting week equals the ELT hires block for that week, with any difference explained line by line. Rollback is a code revert.

### PO1. Offer outcomes (week three)

`analyze_offer_outcomes` is the E1 sketch from the capability map. Reads: offers over the window with no status filter and the full chain, offers with `current_only=true`, rejection details for the rejected offers' applications, and rejection reasons with defaults included. Metrics: acceptance rate per application (accepted current offers over accepted plus rejected current offers), acceptance rate per extension (the same over all rows), decline reasons grouped by the reason's `type.key` and then its name, with an unclassified bucket for offers whose application was never rejected, and decision days from `sent_on` to `resolved_at` as a median and middle half (25th to 75th percentile). Dimensions: department, office, source category, hiring manager. Disclosures: both denominators and the re-extension count between them, rejected offers with no rejection detail, rows missing `sent_on`, the private-candidate exclusion, and the noon-UTC backfill artefact when more than a threshold share of `resolved_at` values sit at exactly 12:00Z. This replaces the planner's `offer_resolution` binding for offer questions. Tests: new `test/offer-outcomes.test.ts`. The gate is the India figure from the TI census reproducing: 48 percent acceptance (36 of 75 resolved offers) and 2.1 offer rows per hire for the same window. Rollback is a code revert.

### PO2. Time to hire and time to fill (week four)

`analyze_time_to_hire_and_fill` is the E2 sketch. From the accepted set, it reads the applications by id, their stage rows in batches of 50 (the batch size stage latency already uses, `S/tools/stage-latency.ts:17`), each job's stage definitions one job at a time (a 50-id batch returns 422), the openings by `application_id`, and the close reasons. Four clocks, each stated in the answer: the candidate clock from application created to offer accepted; the process clock from the recruiter-screen stage's `entered_at` to accepted, resolving the screen stage by exact name per job with `Recruiter Phone Screen` as the default; the job clock from `jobs.opened_at`; and the seat clock from opening opened to opening closed where the close reason starts with `Hire -`. Offer-to-start from `resolved_at` to the current version's `starts_on` rides along. All are calendar days, median and middle half, cohorted by the month the application started and split by office group and source category. Disclosures: hires whose application never entered the screen stage (excluded from the process clock and counted), outbound-sourced hires flagged on the candidate clock as data-entry artefacts, openings with no close reason, cloned jobs whose job clock is a lower bound, and the stage-name list used. Tests: new `test/time-to-hire.test.ts`. The gate is the TI memo's medians reproducing under its cohort definition, US screen-to-start 53.3 days and India 46.7 days for 2026 acceptances. Rollback is a code revert.

### PO3. Hires by period and upcoming starts (week four)

`analyze_hires_by_period` is the E3 sketch: hires per calendar month by department (walking `parent_id` to a top-level group), office (first of `office_ids` as primary, with the multi-office count disclosed) and source category, referral share, and an upcoming-starts mode listing accepted current offers with `starts_on` on or after today by week. Names come from `list_candidates` only when the caller asks and only where P2's gate allows. The reconciliation line prints first. The gate is agreement with the ELT hires block for one week. Effort 1.5 days; rollback a code revert.

### PO4. Open headcount, job age and fill rate (week four)

`analyze_headcount_fill` is the E4 sketch: the demand reconciliation the capacity program specifies (beginning open recruitable openings, plus opened, minus closed by hire, minus closed otherwise, equals ending open), fill rate over closed openings in the window, job age over open jobs, and median seat age over open openings, excluding openings on draft or template jobs and counting closed openings with no close reason as outcome unknown, never as filled. It supersedes the planner's `opening_fill_status` binding. The gate is today's open-job and open-opening counts matching `search_my_jobs` and `search_my_openings` read directly, with the draft-job openings counted separately. Effort 1.5 days; rollback a code revert.

### Q1. The question library (week four)

No vendor publishes the questions its assistant answers, and the external research names that as a differentiator. `packages/recruiter-mcp/docs/question-library.md` lists every recipe with the questions it answers in the words a recruiter or People Leadership would use, its clock, its default window, its denominator and its disclosures, and it is the text the People Ops brief's readers get when they ask what the pack can do. One day, no code, no rollback needed.

### PO5. Hiring-team load and interviewer load (week six)

`analyze_hiring_team_load` is the E5 sketch. Per recruiter (job owners of type recruiter with the responsible flag): open requisitions, open openings, active applications, hires in the window. Per hiring manager: open requisitions and active applications. Per interviewer: dated interviews attended, distinct applications, scorecards submitted over scorecards owed, and median hours from interview to submission, with interview minutes from `search_my_job_interviews` now that R2 exposed it. A team-capacity line gives distinct interviewers and total seats per week. It needs the `show_service_accounts` parameter from R2a to keep service accounts out of denominators, and it removes TA staff from the interviewer panel by their job-owner rows, never by title, since `job_title` is empty across the tenant. Disclosures: assignment is point-in-time, scaffolding statuses excluded with their count, deactivated users on historical rows, agency users separated. Two days of effort, and a code revert rolls it back. The gate is the India coordinator case from the TI census reproducing: one coordinator holding 72 of 253 seats with zero scorecards, and being excluded as TA staff.

### PO9. Approval cycle time (week six)

With the three approval readers exposed by R2a, one day joins flows to approver groups to approvers and reports request-sent to last-group-resolved by department and approval type, replacing the planner's pending-age-only `approval_latency` metric (`S/metrics.ts:222-234`). The gate is the measured 0.1 to 0.3 days between verbal and signature from the TI census reproducing for `offer_candidate` flows.

### PO10. Offer against the posted band (week seven)

One day, conditional on H0's probe finding the base-pay definition not flagged private. Offer compensation custom fields, decoded through `search_my_custom_fields?field_type=offer`, against `search_my_pay_input_ranges` per job post, with `min_cents` and `max_cents` of zero disclosed as a range never filled in. If the definition is private, this recipe does not exist on the recruiter surface, and the reason is the org's own privacy flag.

### PO7 and PO11. Pass-through by stage, and the prospect pipeline (week nine)

Pass-through by stage implements the `stage_conversion_rate` metric that is registered but returns not-implemented (`S/metrics.ts:132-148`), applying the advancement rule from the capacity program (an advance counts only when the next occupied stage is canonically later) over per-job stage definitions, and discloses the stage map it used, since the governed stage map is not finished, which is two days of work. The prospect pipeline is a one-day point-in-time distribution over the three prospect readers R2a exposed; movement over time needs the snapshot Job, which exists unscheduled, and stays labeled as not computed until it runs.

### Deferred, and why

The demographic mode waits on a named request (decision 3). The HRIS join table waits on tier one and a sponsor, and lives in the analytics platform (decision 4). Autonomous scheduling and autonomous outreach are not built: the interview endpoints record events the calendar already holds, so scheduling is not Greenhouse's to do, and outreach is where every published incident lives. Pre-debrief packets and compensation checks are not recipes, because the reads they need exist after R2 and the assistant composes them. Auto-closing requisitions is not built; the assistant reminds, proposes and drafts, and a person applies.

## 8. How each slice is reviewed and shipped

Each slice runs the loop this repo's last three builds ran. The plan is attacked before any code by two read-only lenses, one for correctness and one for scope and rollback, and by the standing seat whose only job is to attack the slice for being too narrow; findings from that seat rank with the others. The code is then attacked by three lenses on the diff: correctness, test honesty (which red-tests the critical assertion against a naive implementation, and which caught the most in PR #2), and data safety for any slice that touches a store or a migration (P2, R3's index cache, every R4 kind). Lenses run at the author's model tier or above, and any parallel agents that mutate files run in their own worktrees. `npm run verify` in `packages/recruiter-mcp` (typecheck, test, guard) and in `packages/action-mcp` (which also builds) is green before merge; the merge SHA is built by Cloud Build from `packages/recruiter-mcp/deploy/cloudbuild.yaml` and deployed as a new revision with the SHA in `GREENHOUSE_RECRUITER_BUILD_SHA`; the live gate named in the slice is run through a hosted-client session against Cloud Run; and the SHA, gate result and any live evidence are recorded on the slice's Linear issue before the next slice starts.

## 9. The thing you're not seeing

Two hosts are live, and every gate in this spec is only meaningful on one of them. Render still serves the pre-OAuth build at `0b1a854` with a 44-tool catalog and immortal legacy tokens, Sam's own Claude Code entry points at it, and the readiness monitor curls it. A live check run from that entry verifies the wrong server. The Render retirement checklist recorded on CLO-269 is not in this spec's nine weeks, but its first three steps (separate Harvest clients, scheduled Jobs, a committed source of truth for the production environment) are the ground every slice's gate stands on, and P4's probe may force the first of them early.

The catalog is about to get expensive. A 44-tool session already costs about 23,000 tokens before anyone types; after R2 an entitled session carries 92 tools, and R1, PO1 through PO11 and R4 add roughly twenty more. R2c buys back a third of that. The durable answer is a per-person surface: the directory row already carries a persona, the runtime already gates tools by surface and by grant (`S/limits.ts:330`), and a coordinator does not need eleven People Ops recipes any more than a People Leadership reader needs 22 write tools. That profile switch is not a slice here, because no slice depends on it, but it is the pressure the build will feel by week six, and it should be planned then rather than discovered.

The pack's numbers will be compared with the analytics platform's published reports in the same meeting, and any disagreement will be charged to the assistant. H0's gate checks one week against the ELT block; the definitions themselves have to be visible for the rest to hold, which is what Q1 is for. Where the two systems must differ, such as the process clock the TI memo used against the candidate clock the ELT does not compute, the answer names the clock so the reader can see why.

Greenhouse's own connector now overlaps the read and write tools this repo ships, with the vendor's audit log behind it. What it does not do is compute a metric, scope an aggregate to the asker, preview a write before it lands, or record who asked for it. Every slice in this spec sits on that side of the line: the People Ops track is analysis the official server cannot produce, R1 and R3 are computed answers over scoped reads, and R4 adds writes only with the preview, fence, attribution and audit that the official connector lacks. If the org enables the official connector, that is the argument for keeping ours, and it should be made in those words when the time comes.

Finally, the Linear workspace is at its issue cap. This spec names 27 slices; until the workspace is upgraded, all of them live as one comment on CLO-269, and the per-slice ledger that section 8 requires has nowhere to go. That upgrade is on Sam's list and is the one operational item in this document that blocks the tracking rather than the code.
