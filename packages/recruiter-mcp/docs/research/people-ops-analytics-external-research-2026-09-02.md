# People Ops analytics over Greenhouse through an assistant: external research

Research date: 2026-09-02. Method: five breadth-first research subagents with a fixed budget of 15 web calls each, plus two adversarial verification passes on the claims that would change the recommendation. Every subagent spent its full budget; none was truncated silently. Claims that could not be corroborated from a second source are marked as unverified in the text rather than softened into prose.

## 1. What People teams measure, and what the standards actually define

The metric vocabulary People Leadership uses is narrower than the recruiting-analytics market suggests, and only a handful of the terms carry a published definition with a start event and an end event. The rest are conventions that vary by vendor, and the variation is large enough to move a number by a week or more.

Time to fill has the firmest definition. SHRM defines it as calendar days from the requisition being opened to the offer being accepted, and its 2021 benchmark data puts the non-executive median at 44 days across 840 organizations, with the middle half (25th to 75th percentile) running from 28 to 73 days. [1] SHRM also measures the segment from position open to approved-to-fill on its own, at a median of 7 days and a mean of 19, which is the clearest evidence that starting the clock at approval rather than opening changes the answer materially. [1] ISO 30414 reportedly starts at approval; that attribution comes from a secondary summary because the standard is paywalled, and it should be treated as unverified. [2] Ashby's 2026 benchmarks, computed from application events rather than self-reports, put median time to first fill at 52 days for junior roles, 63 for mid-level and 71 for senior, with technical roles around 75 days against roughly 60 for business roles. [3]

Time to hire has no SHRM definition at all. The market convention is candidate enters the funnel to candidate accepts, and Ashby is the only vendor that states its clock in public: consideration start to marked hired. [15] Gem's 2024 data gives an average of 41 days, up from 33 in 2021, and 20 interviews per hire, up from 14. [4] The pitfall specific to this metric is the start event for sourced candidates, who have no application date that means "entered the funnel," and re-applications, which Gem finds account for 44 to 46 percent of sourced hires. [4][5]

Offer acceptance rate is defined by SHRM as acceptances divided by offers extended, but the three denominators in use give different answers on the same data. SHRM counts offers extended, which means a rescinded offer counts against the team. Ashby counts entries into the offer stage and removes organization-initiated outcomes, so rescinds and withdrawn offers drop out and the result measures candidate declines only. [6] Most vendor dashboards use the third form, offers resolved in the period, which excludes pending offers. Benchmarks cluster: Gem reports 82 to 84 percent across 2021 to 2025; Ashby's three-year average is 78 percent with an 11-point gap between technical roles at 73 percent and business roles at 84 percent; SHRM's self-reported median is 100 percent, which says more about self-reporting than about acceptance. Referrals accept at 84 to 89 percent in Ashby's data. [3][5][1]

Stage pass-through rates have a cohort form, the share of applications entering a stage that eventually advanced, and a period form, advances out of a stage divided by advances plus rejections in the window. Ashby's 2026 figures by stage class are 35 percent at recruiter screen, 24 percent at onsite, 95 percent after onsite and 81 percent at offer, with referred candidates passing the screen at 52 percent. Gem's cumulative figure is that 8 percent of applicants pass initial screening and 0.5 percent reach an offer, roughly one hire per 200 applications. [3][5]

The remaining metrics on the People Leadership list have no published standard. Offer-to-start lag has no SHRM, ISO, Gem or Ashby figure; SHRM stops at offer-to-acceptance, a median of 2 days. [1] Headcount plan versus fill rate is a finance construct that no standard defines and that vendors report ad hoc; the plan itself never lives in the applicant tracking system. Cost per hire has the ANSI/SHRM standard formula, internal plus external recruiting costs divided by hires, with a 2021 median of $1,244 and a mean of $4,683 (the widely quoted "$4,700"); an ATS holds almost none of the numerator, so anything computed from Greenhouse alone is external cost per hire and must be labeled that way. [1] Quality of hire is a composite with no formula: LinkedIn's 2025 survey of 1,271 recruiting leaders found performance ratings used by 66 percent, new-hire retention by 60 percent and hiring-manager satisfaction by 44 percent, with a one-year retention window in LinkedIn's own analysis, and only 25 percent of respondents confident they can measure it. [7] Recruiter capacity has two incompatible forms, concurrent open requisitions (Gem: 13 to 14 per recruiter) and annual throughput (SHRM: median 20 per year), and the ratio between them is roughly time to fill in years. [5][1]

Every one of these metrics inherits three properties of ATS data that the standards do not mention. Dates on jobs, openings and offers are editable and unversioned, so the start event is whatever the last editor left it. Rejections, rescinds and candidate withdrawals collapse into one rejected status distinguished only by a reason taxonomy. Ownership and source attribution are current-state fields with no history, so a requisition reassigned mid-life is credited entirely to its current owner. A metrics layer that wants numbers comparable to SHRM or Ashby has to snapshot those fields on a schedule and keep its own history, because the system of record will not.

## 2. What Greenhouse's own surfaces cover

Greenhouse splits its analytics into three tiers, and none of them computes a metric through an API. The in-app reports are aggregate, filterable by job, department, office, user, hiring-team role, custom job field, application date, stage and rejection reason, and gated by the viewer's permissions. [8] The Business Intelligence Connector, available on the Plus and Pro tiers, is a nightly export to Redshift or S3 that is complete by 10am Eastern and reflects the previous day. [9] The Harvest API is record-level: applications, stage transitions, offers, openings and the rest, with no metric endpoints, so every rate and duration is rebuilt by the caller. The confirmed complaints on review sites are the mirror image of that split: no custom reports from scratch, slow reporting at scale, and a spreadsheet connector slow enough that reviewers joke about it.

The Harvest v1 and v2 APIs were scheduled for removal on August 31, 2026. [10] As of this research date the documentation still reads in the future tense and still documents v1 endpoints, so whether removal has actually happened is unverified; our server has run on v3 since July and is unaffected either way.

Greenhouse's official MCP server changes the competitive picture for one half of what we built. It was announced on May 7, 2026, entered open beta in the July release notes for the Core, Plus and Pro tiers, and is hosted only on US infrastructure at mcp.us.greenhouse.io with no EU endpoint. [11][12][13] Its permission model has three layers: a Site Admin sets a scope ceiling in the Dev Center, each user consents to data categories when they authorize, and then every call runs on the user's own OAuth token so Harvest applies that user's existing role and permissions. Writes exist once an admin enables them, every DELETE endpoint is permanently blocked, and five actions require explicit human confirmation: rejecting an applicant, hiring an applicant, merging candidates, anonymizing a candidate, and bulk-revoking user permissions. Audit-log events are tagged with an MCP event type, but writes appear under the user's name with no indicator that an AI tool made them. [13] Greenhouse publishes no tool list and no per-tool changelog; the widely repeated count of 36 tools traces to a competitor's write-up and is unverified, and whether any aggregate or demographic tools are enabled is unverifiable without connecting and listing them. [14][13] Everything about the server's documented shape says record lookups and record mutations bounded by Harvest data categories, and Harvest has no report endpoints.

The implication for our server is direct. The record-lookup and write tools we ship now duplicate a vendor-supported server with its own audit log and token model. The aggregate analysis layer, the recruiter-scoped computation over live data, and the notification plane are what the official server does not do, and they are where the People Ops investment belongs.

## 3. How the market frames it

Ashby publishes the most complete metric library, and it is the vocabulary to match. Its report catalog names each template and states the clock and the default window: time to hire runs from consideration start to marked hired, time to offer from consideration start to entering the offer stage, time to fill from the opening's open date to its fill date on closed openings only, and time to start from the hired-at date to the employment start date. Time in process uses the median, cohorted by the month the application started, and the funnel report counts only applications whose consideration has ended so in-flight candidates do not dilute conversion. Ashby also ships three interviewer-side reports that nobody else names: interview load, feedback completion, and feedback calibration, which shows the distribution of each interviewer's recommendations to detect bias. [15]

Across the eight products examined, twelve metrics recur in most of them: time to hire, time to fill (with two different clocks in the market, opening-based and requisition-to-acceptance), stage pass-through, offer acceptance rate, source of hire, time in stage, open headcount and age of open jobs, recruiter productivity, interviewer load, in-product benchmarks, targets versus actual, and quality of hire through a join to the HR system. The common dimensions are department, location, source and source type, job or requisition, recruiter, hiring manager, stage, time grain, and application-start cohort. Level is not a first-class dimension in any ATS catalog; it comes from the HR system. Workday and Rippling have no join problem because candidate, hire and employee are one object graph, which is why quality of hire is a report field for them and an integration project for everyone else.

The AI-analyst pattern that shipped in 2025 and 2026 has three shapes: a chat that returns a built report with filters and visuals applied (Ashby's reporting assistant), a chat agent over all tenant data that can also take actions (Ashby Assistant), and a chat that answers with the same permissions the user has in the product (Gem's AI, HiBob's companion). No vendor publishes a library of the questions its assistant answers; the one concrete example found in the wild, "average time to hire for engineering roles in Q1 2026," comes from a third-party guide. [16][17][18] A published question library keyed to the common metrics would itself be a differentiator.

## 4. Governance for demographic data and for assistants over HR data

The two native ATS assistants, Greenhouse's and Ashby's, converged on the same permission pattern: an admin-set ceiling plus the user's own product permissions enforced on every call. The MCP specification's security guidance adds the must-level rules: per-client consent, no acceptance of tokens issued for another server, no use of sessions as authentication, minimal initial scopes with logged elevation, and an audit trail that names the acting user and client on every call. [19] Anthropic's connector documentation adds two facts that matter for HR data: tool results routed through the connector are retained under Anthropic's standard policy rather than a zero-retention arrangement, and it recommends denylisting write tools for read-only assistants. [20]

The rule for demographic data is aggregate only, with a minimum group size, applied to filters as well as to breakouts so that subtracting two answers cannot recover a small cell. Culture Amp states its minimum reporting group size as usually 5, and hides the next-smallest group when one group has a single participant. [21] A conservative value of 10 appears in health and public-statistics anonymization but was not found stated by any HR vendor within budget, so it should be presented as our own choice rather than an industry figure. [22]

The legal frame is consistent across jurisdictions. In the United States, the Uniform Guidelines require employers to keep records showing the impact of selection procedures by race, sex and ethnic group, for agencies, complainants and respondents, not for operational reporting. [23] EEOC guidance on self-identification requires that it be voluntary, kept separate from the application, kept confidential, not shared with the people making selection decisions, and never used as a basis for a decision. [24] Federal contractors face the same separation requirement under OFCCP rules, and disability self-identification must be kept in a data-analysis file rather than personnel files. [25][26] In the EU, GDPR Article 9 prohibits processing data revealing racial or ethnic origin and other special categories except under named conditions, of which employment law obligations, substantial public interest under Member State law, and statistical purposes are the ones that carry diversity monitoring. [27] The UK's equivalent condition expressly does not apply to measures or decisions about a particular data subject. [28] The exact wording of the UK provision and the OFCCP disability rule could not be fetched and is unverified as to quotation, though the section citations are correct.

Greenhouse's own product embodies the tension. Viewing the in-app EEOC report requires a Site Admin with the user-specific permission to see EEOC and demographic reports, and the report is aggregate only: applicant responses never tie back to candidates or applications. [8] The Harvest v3 API says the opposite in its own reference: the EEOC endpoint returns row-level responses tied to a specific candidate and application, gated on an API scope rather than the in-app permission, and the demographic-answers endpoint is the same class. [29] An analytics layer on Harvest therefore sees what the org's UI permission model hides. Under the project's rule against silently granting access the org's own Greenhouse permissions deny, per-row EEOC and demographic answers are the clearest case in the API of data that must be aggregated with small-cell suppression before it reaches any surface, and the aggregate-only in-app rule is the external constraint to cite.

## 5. What the HR system holds that the ATS never sees

Everything after the hire lives in the HRIS. Greenhouse contributes the hired status, the planned start date on the offer, whatever compensation was captured in offer custom fields, approved openings, and the recruiter and hiring manager on the job. Actual start dates and no-shows, retention at 90 days or a year, onboarding completion, compensation bands, the headcount plan, the manager of record, and termination reasons all come from the HRIS or from finance.

Every native Greenhouse-to-HRIS integration examined is one-directional and drops the Greenhouse identifiers. The BambooHR integration, read directly, fires when a candidate is marked hired and writes name, work and personal email, phones, start date, title, department, office, employment type, and up to four custom fields; nothing flows back, and the title must already exist in BambooHR. [30] Workday's Hire Link pushes hires in the same direction, and Workday's HRIS Link is the only Greenhouse-native path that pulls headcount-adjacent data (requisitions, users, departments, offices) from an HRIS into Greenhouse. [31] HiBob, Rippling, Deel and Personio follow the BambooHR pattern as far as could be determined, with several details unverified.

The APIs themselves expose the lifecycle facts. BambooHR, HiBob, Workday, Rippling, Deel, Gusto and Personio all expose employees with hire dates and terminations; compensation is exposed by most; positions and headcount plans are exposed by Workday and, with unverified endpoints, by HiBob and Personio. Unified HRIS APIs (Merge, Finch, Kombo) normalize start and termination dates but none normalizes the termination reason, positions, or bands, so regretted-versus-non-regretted attrition needs a passthrough to the underlying system. Two operational details recur: HiBob excludes inactive employees by default, so terminated staff vanish from retention math unless the request asks for them, and BambooHR's rate limits are deliberately undocumented. [32][33]

The retention join is the whole problem. "Ninety-day retention of Q2 hires by source" needs a link between Greenhouse hired applications and HRIS employee records, keyed on email and start date with manual fallbacks, because no integration carries the Greenhouse identifier across. That is the same class of problem as the recruiter identity directory already in this repo, and the choice of HRIS matters less than owning that join table.

## 6. Implications for a People Ops recipe pack on the Greenhouse MCP

The evidence points to a pack built in three tiers, ordered by how much of the answer Greenhouse already holds.

The first tier is buildable from data the server already reads, and each recipe should declare its clock and its denominator the way Ashby does. Offer acceptance rate, computed on offers resolved in the window with organization-initiated outcomes removed and reported with both the count and the rate by department, office, source and hiring manager; the recipe must collapse offer versions to one per application and must split rescinds from declines by rejection reason. Time to hire, from application creation to offer acceptance, reported as a median with the middle half, cohorted by application month, with a separate time-to-fill clock from opening opened to opening filled on closed openings only; both must exclude cloned requisitions' migrated history and reopened jobs' closed gaps, which are the two Greenhouse-specific traps already recorded in this repo. Offer-to-start lag and upcoming starts, from acceptance to the latest offer version's start date, labeled as planned starts because the ATS never learns about no-shows. Open headcount and age of open jobs by department and office, counting openings rather than jobs and treating openings closed without a hire as cancelled. Hires by month with department, office, source type and recruiter, using source type and identifier rather than source name. Rejection reasons by stage and department, extending the existing drift recipe.

The second tier needs data the server reads but does not yet expose, or a governance layer. Aggregate demographic funnel by stage, computed from the EEOC and demographic-answers endpoints, requires the aggregate-only rule with a minimum cell size and complementary suppression, a response-rate disclosure on every figure, per-stage denominators, and a hard rule that it never runs on a pool the asking user is currently deciding on; it should be offered as an explicit mode gated on the same Greenhouse permission that gates the in-app report. Interviewer load and feedback calibration extend the existing scorecard recipes and are a differentiator few vendors ship. Approval cycle time needs the approval-flow endpoints that exist in the registry but are not bound to tools.

The third tier needs the HRIS. Retention at 90 days and one year, actual starts and no-shows, offer versus compensation band, and headcount plan versus fill all wait on a candidate-to-employee join table and one HRIS read path, and should not be promised until that table exists.

Two decisions sit above the pack. Every recipe answered for an org-wide actor must run without a scope ceremony, or People Leadership will meet a confirmation prompt on every question; the fix for that is already scoped in this repo. And a published library of the questions the pack answers, in People Leadership's own words, is worth as much as any single recipe, because no vendor ships one.
